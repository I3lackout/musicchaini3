# The Music Discovery Law

<br>

## Every authentic interaction should increase the probability that the next great artist is discovered.

<br>

<br>

<br>

---

## What this means

This is not a tagline. It is an operational principle — a test that every feature, economic mechanism, AI model, and governance decision must pass.

Before shipping anything on MusicChain, ask:

> *Does this increase the chance that authentic music gets discovered?*

If yes → it belongs.  
If no → it probably doesn't.  
If unsure → measure against **successful discoveries per day**.

---

## Why a law, not a mission statement

Mission statements describe intent. Laws describe constraints.

MusicChain has many innovative systems:

| System | Role |
|--------|------|
| **TFPS** (Track Field Pressure) | Observes momentum and community pulse around tracks |
| **UserChain** | Records trust and reputation from authentic participation |
| **McCC / Fuel / Gas / ITC** | Resources that power and reward participation |
| **Live sessions** | Real-time discovery and structured feedback |
| **Discovery Score / Explorer XP** | Gamifies and measures exploration |
| **First 100 Listeners** | Permanent record of early discovery |
| **Discovery DNA** | Taste profiles built from exploration, not just replay |
| **AI systems** | Pattern recognition to assist — not replace — human discovery |

Without a central principle, these can feel like separate features assembled over time.

With the Music Discovery Law, every system has a clear purpose. TFPS exists because measuring field pressure increases discovery probability. UserChain exists because trust makes discovery recommendations more reliable. Fuel exists because participation should cost something real — preventing manufactured engagement from polluting the signal.

## Applying the law

### Example: a new feature proposal — "Daily login bonus"

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Does logging in daily help someone discover new music? | No — it rewards presence, not exploration |
| Does it pass the Music Discovery Law? | **No** |
| Alternative | Reward *first discovery of the day* instead |

### Example: TFPS field pressure display

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Does showing field pressure help listeners find rising artists? | Yes — it surfaces momentum before mainstream visibility |
| Does it pass the Music Discovery Law? | **Yes** |

### Example: bot play detection

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Does removing fake plays help authentic artists get discovered? | Yes — it protects signal integrity |
| Does it pass the Music Discovery Law? | **Yes** |

## The law creates coherence

Most platforms accumulate features until the product feels fragmented. Users can't articulate what the platform *is for* beyond "it plays music."

The Music Discovery Law gives MusicChain something rare: **a coherent design philosophy that scales**.

Every future feature can be evaluated against one question. Every economic mechanism can be traced to one outcome. Every AI model can be justified by one metric.

This is how platforms like Google (PageRank), Bitcoin (Proof of Work), and GitHub (open collaboration) maintained coherence as they grew — not through feature lists, but through a single governing principle.

MusicChain's governing principle is discovery.

---

## What the law does not mean

Clarity requires boundaries.

The Music Discovery Law does **not** mean:

- **Every interaction must discover a new artist.** Replay, comfort listening, and returning to favorites are valid. The law governs platform optimization, not user behavior.
- **Popularity is forbidden.** Artists who earn genuine audiences deserve visibility. The law opposes *popularity as the default optimization target*, not popularity as an outcome of authentic discovery.
- **AI replaces human taste.** AI assists pattern recognition. Humans decide what resonates. The law requires authentic human engagement in the discovery loop.
- **Growth at any cost.** Manufactured engagement that inflates metrics without forming real listener-artist connections violates the spirit of the law, even if it increases daily active users.
